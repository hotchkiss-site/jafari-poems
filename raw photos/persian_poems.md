## Page 67

**Original Persian:**
وقتی دلتنگم به تو می‌اندیشم
یاد تو مربعی‌ست محو و لرزان
در زمینه خاکستری روشن
در این مربع‌ها
من
با بهم زدن پلک‌هایم
گذشته را نقاشی می‌کنم.
بین من و تو
غبار و دیوار است
به سحر این مربع‌ها
من از دیوارها می‌گذرم
در رسیدن به تو
تنها راه گذشتن است
باید چراغ رنگ بدست بگیرم
و در خاکستری‌هایم به دنبال تو بگردم
ای کاش می‌توانستم
یک قطره بیشتر
با سرخ نقاشی کنم...
تهران ۱۳۶۸

**English Translation:**
When I am homesick, I think of you
The memory of you is a faded, trembling square
On a light gray background
In these squares
I
By blinking my eyelids
Paint the past.
Between you and me
There is dust and a wall
By the magic of these squares
I pass through the walls
In reaching you
The only way is to pass
I must take up the lantern of color
And search for you in my grays
I wish I could
Paint one drop more
With red...
Tehran 1368

## Page 68

**Original Persian:**
هذیان بعد از جنگ...
اگر ابرها خاکستری نبودند
باران رنگ دیگری داشت
و اشک‌های تو
سبز کم رنگ بودند.
رنگ جنگ در آه می‌ماند در نگاه می‌ماند
من نفرت از جنگ را در چشم‌ها دیده‌ام
و نفرت از صلح را در دندان‌ها
اگر ابرها خاکستری نبودند
باران رنگ دیگری داشت
می‌دانم چشم‌هایت در مهتاب زیباترند
اما...
باید به پناهگاه برگردیم
تهران ۱۳۶۸

**English Translation:**
Delirium after war...
If the clouds were not gray
The rain would have another color
And your tears
Would be pale green.
The color of war remains in a sigh, remains in a gaze
I have seen the hatred of war in eyes
And the hatred of peace in teeth
If the clouds were not gray
The rain would have another color
I know your eyes are more beautiful in the moonlight
But...
We must return to the shelter
Tehran 1368

امشب

نقاشی کردم.
در دیوارهای توبه تو
سایه تو را شنیدم
من آواز خواندم

آواز پرنده را

تهران، پاییز ۱۳۶۸

**English Translation:**
Tonight
I painted
the voice of my doves.
On the walls of your repentance
I heard your shadow
I sang

and the blue lines of the voice of a woman
whom I love
made my tired song colorful
you and I sang
until the breath of the moonlight turned gray...
In my paintings
I saw your voice
All night... the moonlight
in the square of your laughter
was painting my childhood
I wish it would rain...
I wish it would rain...
The smell of straw-and-clay
makes the bird's song
deeper in color.
Tehran, autumn 1368

## Page 79

*Printed at the head of the page:* نامه‌ای با امضاء به حسین علیزاده — *A letter, signed, to Hossein Alizadeh*
*(This poem runs continuously across pages 79–82; the date appears only at the end, on page 82.)*

**Original Persian:**
آن‌شب باران می‌بارید...
خواب دیدم تو...
در کارِ نواختنِ
نقش‌های رنگینِ یک گلیمِ اصیلی...
و من...
صدایِ سازِ عاشق‌های خراسان را،
نقاشی می‌کنم...
گفتم تو را دوست دارم،
کاش، نقاشی‌های مرا می‌دیدی...
گفتی گوش کن...
و همهٔ نقاشی‌های مرا، ندیده نواختی...
گویی در خواب هذیان می‌گویم... گفتم...
ابر به هوای بوی کاهگل می‌بارد،
و بوی کاهگل نَفَسِ پرنده است...
نَفَسِ پرنده... با سازِ تو... پررنگ‌تر می‌شود....
گفتی:
بوی کاهگل آوازِ پرنده را...
رنگین‌تر می‌کند...
«همیشه آواز برای شنیدن نیست،
گاه خواندن هم چون نمک بر زخم،
برای بیدار ماندن است.»
گفتم: در این شبِ بارانی،
تا آمدنِ تو،
در پنجره، چراغ بیاویزم...

**English Translation:**
That night it was raining...
I dreamed you...
at the work of playing
the colored patterns of a true kilim...
and I...
am painting the sound
of the saz of the ashiqs of Khorasan...
I said I love you,
I wish you could have seen my paintings...
You said listen...
and, unseen, you played every one of my paintings...
As though I were raving in a dream... I said...
The cloud rains out of longing for the smell of straw-and-clay,
and the smell of straw-and-clay is the breath of the bird...
The breath of the bird... with your saz... grows deeper in color....
You said:
The smell of straw-and-clay makes the bird's song...
more colorful...
"Song is not always for hearing,
sometimes singing, like salt on a wound,
is for staying awake."
I said: on this rainy night,
until you come,
let me hang a lamp in the window...

## Page 80

*(Continuation of the letter-poem to Hossein Alizadeh, begun on page 79.)*

**Original Persian:**
گفتی: چراغ نَه،
آوازَت را، مثلِ چِراغ،
در جانِ پنجره بیاویز،
بی‌ترس از باد... و... نگاهِ بد.
آواز تو خاموش نخواهد شد...
آواز تو، شکسته‌تر از آنست،
که باز هم بشکنند...
آواز باید، نفسی تازه باشد...
در هوایی معاصر.
هیچ پرنده‌ای...
آوازِ دیروز را تکرار نمی‌کند.
تهمتِ تکرار... درعادتِ ماست...
عشق، نگاهِ نو را با خود دارد...
تو، در پیِ عناصرِ اصیلِ تازه باش...
من تشنهٔ نگاه معاصر درهوای قدیمی‌ام...
گفتم: من صداهای معاصر،
در فضاهای قدیمی را نقاشی کرده‌ام...
اما، آبی هوای قدیمی را...
به سربی هوای تازه نمی‌بخشم...
ای کاش آن «پیرِ شاعر» هم...
نقاشی‌های مرا نگاه نمی‌کرد...
می‌دید و می‌شنید...
من، بارها... در بامدادِ سرد.... و...
شامگاه درد....
«از پشتِ شیشه‌ها به خیابان نظر کرده‌ام»

**English Translation:**
You said: not a lamp,
hang your song, like a lamp,
in the soul of the window,
unafraid of the wind... and... the evil eye.
Your song will not be put out...
Your song is more broken
than what they could break again...
A song must be a fresh breath...
in contemporary air.
No bird...
repeats yesterday's song.
The charge of repetition... lies in our habit...
Love carries the new gaze with it...
Be after fresh, authentic elements...
I thirst for a contemporary gaze in my old air...
I said: I have painted contemporary sounds
in old spaces...
but the blue of the old air...
I will not give over to the lead-gray of the new...
I only wish that "old poet" too...
had not looked at my paintings...
had seen, and heard...
I have, many times... in the cold dawn.... and...
the evening of pain....
"from behind the window-panes I have gazed out at the street"

## Page 82

*(End of the letter-poem to Hossein Alizadeh, begun on page 79.)*

**Original Persian:**
آهنگ‌هایی نقاشی می‌کردم،
که عثمان، دوتارزنِ جادویی خواف را...
حیران می‌کرد...
گفتم:
من صدای تو را،
و... دیگران را... که تو را دوست دارند،

صداهای معاصر در فضاهای قدیمی،
یا... فضاهای معاصر در صداهای قدیمی...
مثلِ آهنگ‌های تو.
درخواب،
نامِ آخرین ساخته‌هایت را پرسیدم
گفتی:
اولین لالایی برای کودکی عثمانِ خوافی...
و... فراغی شاد... و...
زمزمهٔ چوپانی گنجشک‌های جنگل نور...
و... دلتنگی برای...
آهوان و پرندگان یک فرشِ قدیمی...
و... مرثیه‌ای بزرگ برای...
پنجه‌های کوچکِ دخترانِ قالی‌باف...
از خواب بیدار شدم، آفتاب سرزده بود،
اما... باران هنوز می‌بارید...
تهران آذر ۱۳۶۸

**English Translation:**
I was painting melodies
that left Osman, the magician dotar-player of Khaf...
bewildered...
I said:
I paint your voice,
and... the others... who love you...
contemporary sounds in old spaces,
or... contemporary spaces in old sounds...
like your melodies.
In the dream
I asked the names of your latest compositions.
You said:
"First lullaby for the childhood of Osman of Khaf"...
and... "A joyful Faraghi"... and...
"A shepherd's murmur of the sparrows of the forest of light"...
and... "A longing for
the deer and the birds of an old carpet"...
and... "A great elegy for
the small fingers of the carpet-weaving girls"...
I woke from the dream; the sun had come up,
but... the rain was still falling...
Tehran, Azar 1368

تنها در قفس... در خونش غلطان می‌بینم...
چنان به خشم می‌آیم... که....
کلاغان بر بام نشسته را
ضحّاک می‌پندارم.
من کی‌ام؟...
در مرگِ پرنده‌ام گریه می‌کنم...
از دوری کبوترانم غمناک می‌شوم...
گاه ساعت‌ها...
در کنار دوست شکارچی‌ام
برای شکار آهویی در دشت پرسه می‌زنم...
و خاطره‌ای خوش از شبی دارم...
که گوسفندی را به تمامی...
پوست کندیم...
بر آتش گرداندیم... و... خوردیم...
آنگاه... به شعری لطیف در رثای...
یک قناری زرد گوش دادیم...
برخیزم...

**English Translation:**
One of my doves does not come back,
I make a new poem...
One of my doves dies,
and I pass through a thought... cold... silent...
and... wordless...
One of my sick doves
alone in its cage... I see rolling in its own blood...
Such a rage comes over me... that....
I take the crows perched on the roof
for Zahhak.
Who am I?...
I weep at the death of my bird...
I turn sorrowful at the absence of my doves...
Sometimes for hours...
beside my hunter friend
I roam the plain to hunt a deer...
and I have a fond memory of a night...
when we skinned a sheep...
whole...
turned it over the fire... and... ate it...
And then... we listened to a delicate poem
in elegy for...
a yellow canary...
Let me rise...

## Notes for the next agent

**What this file is.** A page-by-page transcription of photographs of the printed book, kept in
page order under `## Page NN` headings. It is a *grounding dump*, not a rendered artifact — the
poems become real when they are converted into `poems/<slug>.poem` files (see
`../jafari-conversion-skill.md` for that job, and `../CLAUDE.md` for the `.poem` format).

**Where the run stands.** Pages **61–109** are transcribed. Pages 61–69 came from an earlier
source; **70–109** were read from photographs. The next batch of photographs continues at
**page 110**.

Transcription and conversion are at different points, so keep them apart in your head:

- **Pages 61–89 are transcribed *and* converted** into `poems/*.poem` — 61–69 in an earlier pass,
  page 70 as `stay-go`, and 71–89 in July 2026 (16 new files; pages 79–82 recombined into
  `letter-alizadeh`).
- **Pages 90–109 are transcribed *and* converted** (July 2026, 16 files: page 100 was already
  `gone-coming`, pages 102–103 joined as `sparrow-hunter`, 104–105 as `look-again`, 108–109 as
  `not-having`). The next conversion job begins with whatever page 110 onward brings.

**The photo workflow.** New photographs arrive in `raw photos/incoming/`. Transcribe them into
this file, then **move them to `raw photos/processed/`** so `incoming/` is always exactly the
backlog. The images are gitignored (`raw photos/*`, markdown excepted) — bulky, and their content
lives here now, so do not expect them in a fresh clone.

**How to do the next batch.**

1. **See every page.** Read each photo with vision — one page per file, filename order = page
   order. Do not batch-guess from the sequence: **confirm the printed folio digit** in the
   corner of each page against the heading you write. That is the only thing keeping page
   numbers honest.
2. **Dim or blurry photo? Fix it here before asking for a retake.** This recipe resolved page
   73 (the worst so far) without one — crop the text block, then with PIL:
   grayscale → `resize(×3–5, LANCZOS)` → `ImageOps.autocontrast(cutoff=1)` →
   `ImageFilter.UnsharpMask(radius=6–10, percent=220–260, threshold=1–2)` →
   `ImageEnhance.Contrast(≈1.8)`. Crop a single uncertain *line* at ×5 when a word is still
   ambiguous. Work in the scratchpad, not in the repo.
3. **Match the existing shape:** `## Page NN`, then `**Original Persian:**`, then
   `**English Translation:**`, with the poet's date line kept at the foot of each block.
4. **Verbatim.** Keep the ellipses (`...` / `....`), the ezāfe diacritics, and the poet's
   spacing exactly as printed — they carry the rhythm. Flag doubts here; never silently emend.
5. **Anything that is not poem body** — a dedication in the margin, a title above the poem, a
   footnote under the rule, a later hand's marginalia — goes on its own *italic editorial line*,
   not into the verse. When converting, those become `persian_title` / `===footnotes===` /
   meta `notes`, not `===persian===` lines.
6. **A missing date is the signal that a poem continues onto the next page.** Every poem in this
   book ends with a date line. If a page has none, it runs on — check the facing page before
   cutting. (Pages 79–82, 104–105 and 108–109 are each one poem this way.)
7. **Before writing a page up, check whether it is already a `.poem`.** Everything up to page 89
   is converted, and two pages beyond it are too — page 100 is `gone-coming`, already carrying
   Ben's finished translation. Grep `page_number` — though note it is empty in some older files,
   so match on the Persian first line too.

**The English in this file is a literal working pass, not a finished translation.** When you
convert a page to a `.poem`: this English is `===machine===` material, your own interpretive
rendering goes in `===lantern===`, and `===translation===` stays **empty for Ben** unless he
asks you to stand one up (then keep `draft = true`).

**Ben's marginalia.** He reads this book with a pencil, and his own English drafts turn up in the
margins (page 70 in blue ink, page 100 in pencil). These are **his hand, not the poet's and not an
agent's** — record them on an editorial line, and check where they belong before copying them
anywhere: page 70's went into `stay-go`'s `===lantern===`; page 100's *was already* the finished
`===translation===` in `gone-coming`, so it needed nothing. Precedent worth keeping: **lantern can
hold a human draft**, not only agent work.

**Per-page flags:**

- **Page 72, line 4** — printed `در دیوارهای توبه تو`, literally "on the walls of your
  repentance", which sits oddly with the rest of the poem. Possibly a typesetting slip for
  `تو به تو` ("winding walls"). Transcribed verbatim, **not** emended. Ben to adjudicate.
  **Update (July 2026): the manuscript supports the emendation.** The handwritten page that closes
  the book — transcribed in `preface/04-coda-from-jafari.html` — is a variant fair copy of this
  poem, and it reads `در دیوارهای تو به توی سایه`. So the printed `توبه تو` is very likely the
  typesetting slip it looks like. Still not emended in the transcription above; the decision is
  Ben's, and both texts are now on the page for him.
- **Page 72 is the title poem, and it has a twin.** Its last three lines
  (`بوی کاهگل / آواز پرنده را / پررنگ‌تر می‌کند`) are where the book's title comes from, and the
  handwritten signed page at the end of the book is a fair copy of the whole poem. Converted as
  `poems/adobe-birdsong.poem`, cross-linked from the preface coda. The printed text is also the key
  that finally decoded that page's corrupt OCR — `صدای کبوترانم` had been split into
  `صدای کبود ترانم`, `من با دوتار پیری` into `برین باد و کارپوری`, `رنگین` into `زانی`, and
  `بوی کاهگل` itself into `بودی کامنگل`.
- **Pages 79–82** — a single continuous poem (the letter to Hossein Alizadeh) broken across
  four printed pages: heading once on 79, date once at the end of 82. Kept split here to preserve
  the page numbering; **already recombined into one `.poem`, `letter-alizadeh`.** The same
  treatment is still owed to pages 104–105 and 108–109.
- **Page 80** — ends on a line in guillemets, `«از پشتِ شیشه‌ها به خیابان نظر کرده‌ام»`, quoted
  from the "old poet" named two lines earlier. Source unidentified; left in quotation marks.
- **Page 82** — Khorasani music vocabulary: `عثمان` is Osman, the dotar master of **Khaf**
  (خواف, eastern Khorasan); `فراغی` is the *maqam Faraghi*, named for the poet Makhtumkuli
  Fraghi. The four "compositions" the dream-Alizadeh recites are invented titles inside the
  poem — set in quotation marks in the English for legibility, though the Persian has none.
  Osman returns on **page 99**, so the two poems are linked.
- **Page 96, line 13** — `تو با ماهی` is genuinely ambiguous: "you are with the **moon**"
  (ماه + copula) or "you are with the **fish**" (ماهی). Rendered as *moon* to match the poem's
  own lantern-of-the-moon imagery, but the Zayandeh-rud setting makes *fish* readable too.
  Flagged, not resolved.
- **Pages 96 and 97** are a matched Isfahan pair (moon, bridge, the one old tree, the
  Zayandeh-rud) printed **out of date order** — 96 is dated 11/22 and 97 is 11/21. Printed
  sequence kept as-is; this is the book's ordering, not a transcription slip.
- **Pages 102–103** — page 102 carries no date, which by the rule above would make 103 its
  continuation; but 102 ends on a full stop and a complete image, and 103's four lines read
  as a self-contained coda. **Kept as two entries here — but converted as one poem**
  (`sparrow-hunter`). The deciding evidence came from page 95: it ends
  `های... بارانِ من... چقدر ترا دوست دارم`, the identical closing formula page 103 uses
  (`های... آواز... چقدر تو را دوست دارم`). It is one of the poet's closing apostrophes, not a
  standalone poem — and page 103 carries the date page 102 lacks. Easy to split again if Ben
  disagrees.
- **Page 102 is where the book's title appears verbatim.** `با بوی کاهگل و آواز پرنده`, with the
  `و`, exactly as on the cover. Page 72 has the two phrases inside a sentence
  (`بوی کاهگل / آواز پرنده را / پررنگ‌تر می‌کند`) and the dream-Alizadeh repeats that claim on
  page 79 — so the phrase occurs three times in the book, and only here as the phrase itself.
- **Page 102 also settles the `عاشقانه‌های ابری` question.** It is printed
  `... از «عاشقانه‌های ابری»` — "*... from* Cloudy Love Poems" — which means the heading above
  page 88 names a **sequence**, not that poem. `gray-gaze`'s title fields, filled speculatively
  when 88 was converted, have been cleared.
- **Page 104** — quotes `«مسیر نبض عناصر»` and footnotes it, at the foot of page 105, to
  **سهراب سپهری** (Sohrab Sepehri). This is the first real `===footnotes===` material in the
  run; keep it attached to the poem, not folded into the body.
- **Page 108** — `ضحّاک` is **Zahhak**, the serpent-shouldered tyrant of the *Shahnameh*; the
  crows on the roof are being seen as him. Also note the poem says `برخیزم...` at the end of
  108 and `برخیزم همهٔ کبوترانم را` at the top of 109 — read as the poem's own refrain, not a
  printer's catchword (no other page in this run uses catchwords).
- **Page 90 is out of date order.** `۱۳۷۰/۹/۲۷` is 18 December 1991, where pages 91–109 are
  ۱۳۶۸–۱۳۶۹, in a book that is otherwise 99% chronological. Worth confirming the folio and the
  date against the photograph before it is trusted.
- **Day-level dates resolve exactly.** Where the source gives a day (`۱۳۶۸/۱۰/۶`) the Gregorian
  half of `date_written` is computed rather than rounded: 6 Dey ۱۳۶۸ is 27 December 1989, though
  the month-level convention in `../CLAUDE.md` would round Dey to +622 and say 1990. The rounding
  rule is for month-only and season-only dates, which is what it was written for.
- **Dates** — the run is mostly 1368 but climbs into 1369 and, on page 90, **1370**; page 74 is
  `دههٔ ۷۰`, a decade rather than a year, listed as printed. For the Gregorian half of a `.poem`
  `date_written`, use the season/month rule in `../CLAUDE.md` — not a flat +621.
