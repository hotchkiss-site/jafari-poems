## Page 42

*The first line is printed with a space — `بر دارها` — so it reads "on the gallows" (`دار`, gibbet), not the single word `بردارها`. Confirmed on the enhanced crop.*
*(No poem precedes this one on the page; if page 41 carries a second poem below `oldest-song`, check whether this one begins there.)*

**Original Persian:**
بر دارها...
خطّی و آونگی...
و نسیمی که بوی لاشه‌ها را...
تا... دهن‌درّهٔ کفتارها می‌بُرد...
و باد می‌آمد...
و... باد می‌آمد...
آنقدر تند که...
کفتارها مرد مصلوب را...
با موهای بلندش نشناختند...
آنگاه باد...
صلیبِ تنها را از روی تپه بُرد...
۱۳۴۷

**English Translation:**
On the gallows...
linear, and pendulous...
and a breeze that was carrying the smell of the carcasses...
to... the yawning mouths of the hyenas...
And the wind was coming...
and... the wind was coming...
so hard that...
the hyenas did not know the crucified man...
by his long hair...
And then the wind...
carried the lone cross off the hill...
1347

**Original Persian:**
شهر تاریک است،
کوچه‌های خسته در هذیانِ خواب‌آلود
می‌سوزد...
چشمِ شهر از دود می‌سوزد...

و... دروغی را که باید کرد باور...
خنجرِ رنجی‌ست،
جان از این معنا که
ما با رنج می‌سازیم... می‌سوزد
جز دلِ سنگینِ دژخیمان
هرچه در آتش می‌اندازیم می‌سوزد...
موسِمِ خواری‌ست...
موسمی که معنی فریاد آشوب است...
غنچه‌ها می‌پژمرد در سردی دل‌ها...
و...گلِ فریاد بر لب‌ها نمی‌روید...
موسِمِ خواری‌ست...
هر که بر لب قفل دارد
دست‌هایش شاخ پرباری‌ست.
شهر... دشتِ تشنه را مانَد

**English Translation:**
The city is dark,
the weary alleys, in a drowsy delirium,
are burning...
The city's eye burns from the smoke...
The constables have kindled a fire
with the brushwood of ignorance —
a fire of which they have said...
This is the everlasting light...
Everlastingly, the waking fire is the sun...
And... the lie that one is made to believe...
is a dagger of pain,
the soul burns at this meaning,
that we build with suffering... it burns
— except the stony heart of the hangmen,
whatever we throw into the fire burns...
It is the season of humiliation...
a season whose meaning is the cry of riot...
The buds withered in the cold of hearts...
and... the flower of the cry does not grow on the lips...
It is the season of humiliation...
whoever keeps a lock on his lips,
his hands are a laden bough.
The city... has come to resemble a thirsty plain

## Page 45

*This poem is already in the repo as `poems/city-smoke.poem`, where it had been recorded as **page 25** — from a marker in `raw_Jafari-adobe-2.txt`. The photograph puts it on 45–46. The Persian below is the printed text.*
*(This page carries no date; the poem ends on page 46. The blank line before `در شبانِ پر هراسِ شهر` is in the print.)*
*`چَم` in `در چنین چَم کبود` is pointed with a fatha in the print and is not obvious: a bend or meander (as in `چم و خم`) rather than "sky", which is how the machine pass read it. Flagged, not resolved.*

**Original Persian:**
از کنار دشت...
از جوارِ کوه...
از زلالِ آبی قدیم پرشکوه...
من به شهرِ دود آمدم...
من به شهرِ پرصدای بی‌سرود...
نه... به شهرِ بی امیدِ خستهٔ خمود آمدم...
عمر می‌گذشت...
آه می‌وزید...
مارِ خستگی... دست چیدن مرا...
چشمِ دیدنِ مرا...
پای رفتنِ مرا به راهِ عشق می‌گزید...
در چنین چَم کبود... در چنین غروبِ غم...
در چنین خمارِ خستهٔ خمود...
وای اگر خیالِ تو نبود...
برقِ چشمهٔ زلالِ تو نبود...
وای اگر صدای تو نبود... رازِ تو نبود...
زخمه‌های سازِ تو نبود...
بازوانِ باز تو نبود...

در شبانِ پر هراسِ شهر...
مردِ خسته‌ای در آرزوی ماه بود...
زیرِ پای سایه‌های غم، کوچهٔ تو غرقِ آه بود...
در غروبِ این ستاره‌ها...
در صدایِ زخمدارِ این مناره‌ها...

**English Translation:**
From beside the plain...
from the flank of the mountain...
from the clear old glorious blue...
I came to the city of smoke...
I came to the city full of noise and without a song...
No... I came to the city without hope, weary and slumped...
Life was passing...
A sigh was blowing...
The snake of exhaustion... my plucking hand...
my seeing eye...
my walking foot on the road of love — it bit them all...
In such a livid bend... in such a sunset of grief...
in such a weary, slumped stupor...
Alas, if the thought of you had not been...
if the gleam of your clear spring had not been...
Alas, if your voice had not been... your secret had not been...
the plectrum-strokes of your music had not been...
your open arms had not been...

Through the terror-filled nights of the city...
a weary man went longing for the moon...
Under the feet of the shadows of grief, your alley was drowned in sighs...
In the setting of these stars...
in the wounded voice of these minarets...

**English Translation:**
When I am homesick, I think of you
The memory of you is a faded, trembling square
On a light gray background
In these squares
I
By blinking my eyelids
Paint the past.
Between you and me
There is dust and a wall
By the magic of these squares
I pass through the walls
In reaching you
The only way is to pass
I must take up the lantern of color
And search for you in my grays
I wish I could
Paint one drop more
With red...
Tehran 1368

**What this batch is, as a body of work.** Pages 110–199 carry the book from ۱۳۶۹ to ۱۳۸۶ — 1990 to

**The photo workflow.** New photographs arrive in `raw photos/incoming/`. Transcribe them into
this file, then **move them to `raw photos/processed/`** so `incoming/` is always exactly the
backlog. The images are gitignored (`raw photos/*`, markdown excepted) — bulky, and their content
lives here now, so do not expect them in a fresh clone.

**How to do the next batch.**

1. **See every page.** Read each photo with vision — one page per file, filename order = page
   order. Do not batch-guess from the sequence: **confirm the printed folio digit** in the
   corner of each page against the heading you write. That is the only thing keeping page
   numbers honest.
2. **Dim or blurry photo? Fix it here before asking for a retake.** This recipe resolved page
   73 (the worst so far) without one — crop the text block, then with PIL:
   grayscale → `resize(×3–5, LANCZOS)` → `ImageOps.autocontrast(cutoff=1)` →
   `ImageFilter.UnsharpMask(radius=6–10, percent=220–260, threshold=1–2)` →
   `ImageEnhance.Contrast(≈1.8)`. Crop a single uncertain *line* at ×5 when a word is still
   ambiguous. Work in the scratchpad, not in the repo.
3. **Match the existing shape:** `## Page NN`, then `**Original Persian:**`, then
   `**English Translation:**`, with the poet's date line kept at the foot of each block.
4. **Verbatim.** Keep the ellipses (`...` / `....`), the ezāfe diacritics, and the poet's
   spacing exactly as printed — they carry the rhythm. Flag doubts here; never silently emend.
5. **Anything that is not poem body** — a dedication in the margin, a title above the poem, a
   footnote under the rule, a later hand's marginalia — goes on its own *italic editorial line*,
   not into the verse. When converting, those become `persian_title` / `===footnotes===` /
   meta `notes`, not `===persian===` lines.
6. **A missing date is the signal that a poem continues onto the next page.** Every poem in this
   book ends with a date line. If a page has none, it runs on — check the facing page before
   cutting. (Pages 79–82, 104–105 and 108–109 are each one poem this way.)
7. **Before writing a page up, check whether it is already a `.poem`.** Everything up to page 89
   is converted, and two pages beyond it are too — page 100 is `gone-coming`, already carrying
   Ben's finished translation. Grep `page_number` — though note it is empty in some older files,
   so match on the Persian first line too.

**The English in this file is a literal working pass, not a finished translation.** When you
convert a page to a `.poem`: this English is `===machine===` material, your own interpretive
rendering goes in `===lantern===`, and `===translation===` stays **empty for Ben** unless he

**What pages 42–46 settled.** They were photographed to fill what looked like an eighteen-year
hole, and instead they closed it: 41–47 now runs ۱۳۴۵، ۱۳۴۷، ۱۳۴۷، ۱۳۴۷، ۱۳۵۱ without a gap. Which
means **pages 47 and 48 are adjacent and nine years apart** — ۱۳۵۲–۱۳۵۹, the late Pahlavi years, the
revolution and the war's outbreak, are absent from the book itself rather than from the photography.
They cannot be behind page 22 either, since 22–40 are ۱۳۳۷–۱۳۴۲. See `../docs/chronology.md`.

Two of the five pages were poems already in the repo under wrong page numbers, both taken from
scanner footers in the Adobe dumps. Dates in this book come from the poet's own line at the foot of
the poem; page numbers come from footers. **Doubt the page number first.**

**What pages 110–199 settled.**

- **The page-72 crux is all but closed.** Page 72's `در دیوارهای توبه تو` has long looked like a
  typesetting run-together of `تو به تو`, "fold within fold." **Page 160 prints `تُو به تُو` plainly,
  with the damma marked on both** — the poet using the phrase elsewhere in the same book. With the
  handwritten fair copy in `preface/04-coda-from-jafari.html` (`در دیوارهای تو به توی سایه`) that is
  two independent witnesses. Still Ben's call, but the case is now strong; nothing has been emended.
- **The poet was born ۱۳۱۸ / 1939.** `پنجاه و پنج بهار` on page 159 (Azar ۱۳۷۳) is the one exact
  reckoning in the book. **Do not date him from `شصت سال`** — "sixty years" appears on pages 163,
  181, 183 and 190, nine years apart, so it is his round figure for *a whole life*.
- **The book runs to ۱۳۸۶ (2008), not ۱۳۸۰.** Page 199 is `زمستان ۱۳۸۶`, which resets what the
  remaining pages can contain and sharpens the one open inversion — see `../docs/chronology.md`.
- **The rate of travel is ~5 pages a year, not 9.** 89 pages between ۱۳۶۹ and ۱۳۸۶.

**A poem without a date line runs onto the next page — this batch proved the rule five times.**
Pages 133, 150, 165, 169 and 184 each carry no date, and each finishes on the facing page. Nothing
else is needed to find a continuation; check for the date line first, before reading for sense.

**Two poems name their dedicatee inside the poem, in a word that is also a common noun.** Page 174
is for فیروزه صابری and turns the mirror `فیروزه‌ای` — turquoise — as its last colour. Pages 184–185
are built on `عاطفه`, tenderness, and only the printed footnote reveals that Atefeh was the nurse's
name. **Look for this before rendering a dedication poem**: the name may be doing work in the body,
and an English that translates the common noun loses the person.

**Persian couplets are set in two columns, and they read across.** Page 197 is one poem printed as
two columns of hemistichs — right column first half-line, left column second. Read down either column
alone and you get plausible nonsense. This is the first page in the run typeset that way; expect
more as the formal poems increase.

- **Page 73, first word — resolved.** This page had been transcribed `های... یاران...` ("hey...
  friends...") against the Adobe dump's `های... باران...` ("hey... rain..."), one letter apart. Ben
  checked the page: it is **`باران`**, rain, and the transcription above is corrected. The poem's own
  logic had pointed that way — the next line is `تو می‌باری`, "you are raining", and page 95 addresses
  the beloved as `بارانِ من` — and it also removes an addressee shift that had looked odd: the poem
  speaks to the rain from first line to last. Merged into `poems/rain-forgotten.poem`.

**Per-page flags:**

- **Page 72, line 4** — printed `در دیوارهای توبه تو`, literally "on the walls of your
  repentance", which sits oddly with the rest of the poem. Possibly a typesetting slip for
  `تو به تو` ("winding walls"). Transcribed verbatim, **not** emended. Ben to adjudicate.
  **Update (July 2026): the manuscript supports the emendation.** The handwritten page that closes
  the book — transcribed in `preface/04-coda-from-jafari.html` — is a variant fair copy of this
  poem, and it reads `در دیوارهای تو به توی سایه`. So the printed `توبه تو` is very likely the
  typesetting slip it looks like. Still not emended in the transcription above; the decision is
  Ben's, and both texts are now on the page for him.
- **Page 72 is the title poem, and it has a twin.** Its last three lines
  (`بوی کاهگل / آواز پرنده را / پررنگ‌تر می‌کند`) are where the book's title comes from, and the
  handwritten signed page at the end of the book is a fair copy of the whole poem. Converted as
  `poems/adobe-birdsong.poem`, cross-linked from the preface coda. The printed text is also the key
  that finally decoded that page's corrupt OCR — `صدای کبوترانم` had been split into
  `صدای کبود ترانم`, `من با دوتار پیری` into `برین باد و کارپوری`, `رنگین` into `زانی`, and
  `بوی کاهگل` itself into `بودی کامنگل`.
- **Pages 79–82** — a single continuous poem (the letter to Hossein Alizadeh) broken across
  four printed pages: heading once on 79, date once at the end of 82. Kept split here to preserve
  the page numbering; **already recombined into one `.poem`, `letter-alizadeh`.** The same
  treatment is still owed to pages 104–105 and 108–109.
- **Page 80** — ends on a line in guillemets, `«از پشتِ شیشه‌ها به خیابان نظر کرده‌ام»`, quoted
  from the "old poet" named two lines earlier. Source unidentified; left in quotation marks.
- **Page 82** — Khorasani music vocabulary: `عثمان` is Osman, the dotar master of **Khaf**
  (خواف, eastern Khorasan); `فراغی` is the *maqam Faraghi*, named for the poet Makhtumkuli
  Fraghi. The four "compositions" the dream-Alizadeh recites are invented titles inside the
  poem — set in quotation marks in the English for legibility, though the Persian has none.
  Osman returns on **page 99**, so the two poems are linked.
- **Page 96, line 13** — `تو با ماهی` is genuinely ambiguous: "you are with the **moon**"
  (ماه + copula) or "you are with the **fish**" (ماهی). Rendered as *moon* to match the poem's
  own lantern-of-the-moon imagery, but the Zayandeh-rud setting makes *fish* readable too.
  Flagged, not resolved.
- **Pages 96 and 97** are a matched Isfahan pair (moon, bridge, the one old tree, the
  Zayandeh-rud) printed **out of date order** — 96 is dated 11/22 and 97 is 11/21. Printed
  sequence kept as-is; this is the book's ordering, not a transcription slip.
- **Pages 102–103** — page 102 carries no date, which by the rule above would make 103 its
  continuation; but 102 ends on a full stop and a complete image, and 103's four lines read
  as a self-contained coda. **Kept as two entries here — but converted as one poem**
  (`sparrow-hunter`). The deciding evidence came from page 95: it ends
  `های... بارانِ من... چقدر ترا دوست دارم`, the identical closing formula page 103 uses
  (`های... آواز... چقدر تو را دوست دارم`). It is one of the poet's closing apostrophes, not a
  standalone poem — and page 103 carries the date page 102 lacks. Easy to split again if Ben
  disagrees.
- **Page 102 is where the book's title appears verbatim.** `با بوی کاهگل و آواز پرنده`, with the
  `و`, exactly as on the cover. Page 72 has the two phrases inside a sentence
  (`بوی کاهگل / آواز پرنده را / پررنگ‌تر می‌کند`) and the dream-Alizadeh repeats that claim on
  page 79 — so the phrase occurs three times in the book, and only here as the phrase itself.
- **Page 102 also settles the `عاشقانه‌های ابری` question.** It is printed
  `... از «عاشقانه‌های ابری»` — "*... from* Cloudy Love Poems" — which means the heading above
  page 88 names a **sequence**, not that poem. `gray-gaze`'s title fields, filled speculatively
  when 88 was converted, have been cleared.
- **Page 104** — quotes `«مسیر نبض عناصر»` and footnotes it, at the foot of page 105, to
  **سهراب سپهری** (Sohrab Sepehri). This is the first real `===footnotes===` material in the
  run; keep it attached to the poem, not folded into the body.
- **Page 108** — `ضحّاک` is **Zahhak**, the serpent-shouldered tyrant of the *Shahnameh*; the
  crows on the roof are being seen as him. Also note the poem says `برخیزم...` at the end of

  printer's catchword (no other page in this run uses catchwords).
- **Page 90 is out of date order.** `۱۳۷۰/۹/۲۷` is 18 December 1991, where pages 91–109 are

  date against the photograph before it is trusted.
- **Day-level dates resolve exactly.** Where the source gives a day (`۱۳۶۸/۱۰/۶`) the Gregorian

  the month-level convention in `../CLAUDE.md` would round Dey to +622 and say 1990. The rounding
  rule is for month-only and season-only dates, which is what it was written for.
- **Dates** — the run is mostly 1368 but climbs into 1369 and, on page 90, **1370**; page 74 is
  `دههٔ ۷۰`, a decade rather than a year, listed as printed. For the Gregorian half of a `.poem`
  `date_written`, use the season/month rule in `../CLAUDE.md` — not a flat +621.
