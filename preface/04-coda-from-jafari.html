English
Tonight I painted the bruise-blue sound of my song.
On the fold-within-fold walls of the shadow —
when did I come to know you?
I sang.
On this wind and ⟨…⟩ I sang.
And the sound ⟨…⟩ of the blue line — the one I love —
my tired song ⟨…⟩
⟨…⟩
all night a glimmer ⟨…⟩ in the square of the desert's laughter.
I painted my childhood.
If only it would rain.
The smell of adobe,
the song of a bird, make ⟨…⟩ more colored.
[ read from the manuscript — the passages marked ⟨…⟩ are unread, not elided ]
Translator’s note

This page is in Jafari's own hand, and it is where the book gets its name. Its last three lines are بوی کاهگل / آواز پرنده — the smell of adobe, the song of a bird — which is the title on the cover. The poem opens by painting the sound of a song and closes with the things that deepen a color, so the whole page is held inside the act of painting, and the title is its last breath.

The Persian here is read directly off a photograph of the manuscript rather than run through OCR, and it is provisional: the hand is a fast shekasteh cursive, one word in the sixth line is struck through by the poet himself, and ⟨…⟩ marks what could not be read with confidence. A fluent reader of the hand would very likely recover more.

Four things the manuscript settles. کی ترا شناختم is first person — "when did I come to know you?" — as the grammar had suggested it must be. خط آبی رنگ is the blue line, a painter's line, which the OCR had mangled to خط آلی. کودکی‌ام را نقاشی کردم — "I painted my childhood" — answers the poem's first line. And کاش باران ببارد, "if only it would rain," appears once, not twice; the doubling was an artifact.

What is gone: گاتهام‌ها ("Gothams"), کامنگل, کارپوری, گردان نان‌های ناز. None of them are on the page. They were a transcriber guessing at strokes, and the English built on them — "the spinning sweet breads," "Persian and the green line," "You were like a brazier" — described a poem that does not exist. The two conjectures offered earlier, جوانی for زانی and شب‌تابی for شب تابی, are withdrawn: the manuscript supports neither.
