English
Tonight I painted the voices of my doves.
On the fold-upon-fold walls of the shadow —
when did I come to know you?
I sang.
I sang with an aged dotar,
and the blue lines of a woman's voice — a woman I love —
gave my tired song its colour.
You and I sang
until the breath of the moonlight went gray...
⟨…⟩
All night... the moonlight, in the square of your laughter,
was painting my childhood.
If only it would rain...
If only it would rain...
The smell of adobe
deepens the colour
of the bird's song.
→ adobe-birdsong [ read from the manuscript against its printed twin — the passage marked ⟨…⟩ is unread, not elided ]
Translator’s note

This page is in Jafari's own hand, and it is where the book gets its name. Its last three lines are بوی کاهگل / آواز پرنده را / پررنگ‌تر می‌کند — the smell of adobe deepens the colour of the bird's song — and the title on the cover is lifted from them. Note that the Persian is not a pairing of two nice things but a claim about one acting on the other. The same sentence comes back in the long dream-letter to Hossein Alizadeh, where Alizadeh is the one who says it.

The page is a fair copy, in his hand, of the poem printed on page 72 of the book, which stands in this collection as adobe-birdsong. Reading the two together resolves nearly all of what was unreadable here: the printed text is what identifies صدای کبوترانم (the voices of my doves), دوتار پیری (an aged dotar) and رنگین (gave it colour). Two places where this manuscript departs from the print are kept as the manuscript has them — کی ترا شناختم, a line the printed page does not carry, and تو به توی, "fold upon fold," which is very likely the answer to a crux in the print, where the same phrase is set as توبه تو and reads as "the walls of your repentance."

An earlier English here, built on a corrupt OCR of this page, opened "tonight I painted the bruise-blue sound of my song." That was a mirage: صدای کبوترانم, the voices of his doves, had been split into صدای کبود ترانم. In the same way من با دوتار پیری became برین باد و کارپوری, رنگین became زانی, and بوی کاهگل — the book's own title — became بودی کامنگل. The doubled کاش باران ببارد, which had been dismissed here as a scanning artifact, is real: the poet writes the line twice.
